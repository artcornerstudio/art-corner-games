## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

The seed idea as written cannot support a $150 price point, because it is aimed at an audience spanning children to seniors — a group priced against free YouTube. Narrowed to **working artists and art-adjacent service providers who already have 4+ inbound inquiries a month and handle them by hand**, the same idea supports a real ladder. The mechanism is **THE STUDIO LINE**: a hard boundary where AI works the desk (intake, quoting, deposits, delivery) and never the easel (concept, taste, hands, judgment, the price). The video is **"I Automated Every Part of My Commissions Except the Art"** — clickable through tension, self-qualifying through the word *commissions*. The funnel runs **free Leak Audit → $150 Build Room → $497 Full Line**, and the $150 room is explicitly a proof factory, not a revenue line.

## Market category

The seed straddles three markets with different price ceilings:

| Category | Buyer | Price ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **Art education** (learn to make art) | Hobbyist, parent, retiree | **Low.** Anchored against free YouTube and $20 Skillshare. |
| **Creative business operations** | Working artist, commission illustrator, POD seller | **Medium.** Buys when a bottleneck is bleeding money. |
| **AI-for-service-business systems** | Consultant, service provider, entrepreneur | **High.** $150 is trivially cheap here. |

The brief's audience spans all three. The $150 workshop is priced for category 3 and marketed to categories 1–3 simultaneously. **That is the central structural problem, larger than any scoring line below.**

## Demand pattern — structural signals

- **S1 — Demand is defensive before it is entrepreneurial.** The dominant charge around AI + art is threat, not appetite. "Human creativity first, AI only for logistics" is the *disarming* answer — the price of entry to be heard, not a differentiator.
- **S2 — The pain is admin, not art.** Unpaid labor sits in commission intake, scope negotiation, quoting, deposit chasing, revision rounds, client updates, delivery, licensing, listing copy, scheduling.
- **S3 — "Build an art company with AI" is a category label, not a search.** People in pain search their bottleneck, not their category.
- **S4 — Downstream of a saturated, low-trust adjacent niche.** "Make money with AI art / POD" slop makes attention cheap and trust expensive. That combination is bad for a cold $150 CTA and good for a free lead magnet into a warmed sequence.
- **S5 — Live is the wrong format for the broad audience, right for the narrow one.** Live pricing works on people whose time is worth more than $150/session.

## Audience psychology

**Sophistication: LOW on AI, MEDIUM-HIGH on craft, LOW on business systems.** The buyer is expert at their work and novice at operationalizing it. They do not need taste taught. They need proof a system exists that will not touch the part of the work they are proud of.

**What they believe:** automation = generic and soulless; AI art = theft and reputational risk; the bottleneck is time but they self-diagnose it as bad marketing; they have bought a course before and not finished it.

**What moves them:** watching the boring part disappear while the art stays untouched; a number (hours reclaimed, dollars recovered); a real pipeline, not a demo.

**Instant sale-killers:** any frame where AI generates the artwork; prompt-box screen recordings with no revenue on screen.

**Segment verdict:** Children, teens and seniors are **audience, not market**. The paying subset is working/commissioning artists and art-adjacent service providers who already have inbound demand handled manually.

## Strategic recommendation

**Cut the audience. Keep the positioning. Narrow the demo. Delay the price.**

1. Redefine the market to *working artists, illustrators and art-service providers who already have inbound requests handled by hand.* Children/teens/seniors remain reach, never the offer's target.
2. Shrink the video from "build an art company" to **one visible system**: commission intake → quote → deposit → delivery. Bottleneck-shaped, screen-recordable, revenue literally on screen, and it proves "AI never touches the art" *structurally* rather than rhetorically.
3. Lead with reclaimed hours and recovered revenue. AI is the mechanism, not the promise.
4. Do not sell the workshop from the video. Sell the lead magnet.
5. Build proof before price (G4).
6. Pre-sell rather than pre-produce (G5).

# 2. OFFER ARCHITECTURE
*Agent: `offer-architect`*

**Buyer (narrowed, non-negotiable):** commission painters and illustrators, muralists, portrait and event photographers, tattoo artists, ceramicists and makers taking custom orders, framers, studio and class owners. **Minimum qualifier: 4+ inquiries/month.** Below that they have a demand problem, not an admin problem.

## Positioning

> I help commissioned artists and studio owners who get more inquiries than they can answer turn every inquiry into a quoted, deposited, scheduled job — **without touching the artwork, hiring an assistant, or learning to code** — by building the Studio Line: a back office where AI runs the paperwork and never crosses into the work.

**Market position:** *This is not an art class. It is a back-office install for people who already sell art.* Price ceiling follows the category you name, so name it in the first sentence, every time.

**Language rules — the defensive-emotion filter:**

| Never lead with | Always lead with |
|---|---|
| "AI for artists" | "Stop losing commissions in your inbox" |
| "automate your art business" | "get every inquiry quoted the same day" |
| "AI tools you need in 2026" | "the 6 hours a week you're spending on paperwork" |
| prompt boxes, models, tools | inquiries, quotes, deposits, delivery |

"AI only for logistics" is not a headline. It is the reassurance given *after* they lean in — and it should be **shown**, not claimed.

## Core offer

### THE STUDIO LINE BUILD ROOM
**$150 · 90 minutes · LIVE · capped at 30 seats · no passive attendance**

**Promise:** You arrive with an inbox. You leave with a working intake link — your prices, your deposit percentage, your revision policy inside it — pasted into your bio before you go to bed.

| Minutes | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | **The Leak Number.** Each person names monthly inquiry count and average job value on screen. Anchors everything. |
| 10–25 | **Gate 1 — Intake.** Their own scope questions: size, surface, deadline, usage rights, revision count. |
| 25–55 | **Gate 2 — Quote.** Their real price list goes in. AI formats and sends; it never decides the number. |
| 55–80 | **Gate 3 — Deposit.** Payment link wired. Live test transaction. Everyone sees money hit an account. |
| 80–90 | **Ship.** Link goes live. Gate 4 handed over as template, framed as the next step. |

**Included:** the **Studio Line Kit** (pre-built intake form, quote logic, deposit gate, plus the written language — scope questions, deposit terms, revision policy, late-client script, delivery and licensing note); the **Leak Audit** worksheet, scored before arrival; a one-page **Data Boundary Map**; and 14 days of Kit support in a build thread that closes.

### Why $150 is premium, not cheap-course-priced

1. **Priced against the leak, not against courses.** One commission lost to a nine-day reply is $400–$2,000. The Leak Audit makes the buyer compute their own number *before* they see the price. A buyer holding "$2,400 leaked last quarter" does not compare $150 to a $20 Skillshare class. **This sequencing is the entire fix for the willingness-to-pay problem.**
2. **You leave with a deployed asset, not knowledge.** The deliverable is a URL, not a notebook.
3. **Why live, three defensible reasons:** it must be configured to *their* prices; the dominant emotion is defensive and only a live room lets someone ask "is this going to touch my art" and get answered in front of everyone; a live deadline is the only mechanism that reliably gets a thing *shipped*.
4. **Premium scaffolding:** capped room, a qualifying question at checkout, no passive replay sold, and a hard guarantee — **leave without a live link and a test transaction and you don't pay.** Turning people away at the door is a price signal no discount can buy back.

Say it out loud on the sales page: *"$150 is deliberately cheap. It is cheap because the room is where I find the ten people who need the whole line built for them."* Naming the strategy makes the price read as confidence rather than as a ceiling.

## Unique mechanism

# THE STUDIO LINE

**Studio Side — 100% human, permanently.** Concept, composition, taste, colour, hands, judgment, the client relationship, **the price**. AI never crosses, and the system is built so it *structurally cannot* — there is no image model in it anywhere.

**Desk Side — 100% systemized.** Intake, scope capture, quoting, deposits, scheduling, client updates, revision tracking, delivery, licensing, listing copy, follow-up.

**The Four Gates — nothing skips a gate:**

| Gate | Name | What it kills |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | Intake | "Can you do something for me?" arriving in five DMs across three weeks |
| **G2** | Quote | Guessing a number at midnight, then eating scope creep |
| **G3** | Deposit | Chasing money, starting unpaid, ghosted clients |
| **G4** | Delivery | Endless revisions, unclear usage rights, no repeat sale |

**Why it's ownable:** every competitor sells "AI for artists" — a tool list. The Studio Line sells a *boundary* plus an *order of operations*. The buyer's real fear is not "will this work," it's "will this take the part of me I'm proud of." The Line is the answer; the Gates are the build.

**Signature line:** *"AI works the desk. You work the easel. The line does not move."*

## The compounding asset — solving R4

**Rule: the Build Room is not the business. It is a proof factory and a diagnostic.** Never run a room that does not produce all four:

1. **The Kit gets one version better.** Built once, sold forever; turns a 40-hour custom install into a 6-hour configuration. Largest margin lever in the business.
2. **The Deposit Ledger.** Checkout checkbox: *"I agree to report my first deposit collected through my Studio Line."* Every entry is simultaneously a case study, a testimonial, a price-raise justification, and the asset that permanently closes R2. **Build it from run one.**
3. **Proof footage.** Every room recorded with consent — real objections, real builds, real transactions. Ninety days of this is the self-serve product and the ad library.
4. **A volume-segmented buyer list.** The room forces every attendee to state inquiry volume on screen. The Build Room's true product is **qualification at scale** — the cheapest way in the market to find the people worth $2,500.

**Price ratchet:** runs 1–3 at $150 to fill the Ledger. Run 4+ at $297 with receipts on the page. **Exit from the trap:** by run six, the recorded build-along plus the Kit is a $197 self-serve product that runs with zero Jean.

## Proof requirements — solving R2 with zero case studies

Ship all six before the first ticket is sold. At $150 the buyer is buying **that the system exists**, not that it worked for 400 people.

1. **The 90-second unedited run.** One continuous recording: inquiry → quote in seconds → deposit link → **payment notification with a real dollar amount visible.** Charge your own card $1 if you must. Real money landing beats any testimonial.
2. **Case Zero: your own studio, 14 days.** Run the Line on ART CORNER's real inquiries. Claim only what you measured, labeled self-reported. *"I did this to my own business first"* is the strongest asset available to a founder with no students.
3. **Three pilot artists, free, for footage.** Ask for **process** claims, not outcomes: *"I had a working quote link in 40 minutes and I've never written a line of code."* Honest, gettable in a week, and it dismantles "I'm not technical."
4. **The Negative Proof.** 60 seconds opening every component on screen showing no image generator anywhere. *"Here is the whole system. Nothing in it can make a mark."* Kills the #1 sale-killer at the top of the funnel instead of arguing in comments.
5. **The Receipt Wall.** Deposit notifications from Case Zero and pilots. Amount visible, client blurred.
6. **The Guarantee, published in writing.** With no track record, a guarantee **is** the proof — converting the buyer's risk into yours, in public.

**Standing rules:** never claim an unmeasured result; never publish a prompt-box recording; revenue-lift language unlocks only when the Deposit Ledger has real entries.

## Lead magnet — the decision

**One magnet, two payloads, fixed order: diagnosis before price, diagnosis before tool.**

The **Leak Audit** is the front door. The **G1 Intake form** — the literal artifact built on screen at 6:00 — is the delivery payload. The audit earns the right to hand over the tool, and the tool is the prescription the audit writes. One URL, one flow, one asset.

*Why not the reverse:* a defensive buyer will not accept a solution before they accept a problem. Hand a "free AI intake template" to someone who believes AI is theft and you get a closed tab. Hand them a number saying their own inbox cost them $3,180 and the tool becomes the bandage, not the threat.

### THE LEAK AUDIT
*Find out what your inbox cost you last quarter. Then close the first gate for free.*

> Twelve questions. Six minutes. One number — the dollars that died between "I'd love to commission you" and money in your account last quarter. Then you get the exact intake form built in the video, with your prices in it. **Nothing in it generates art. Nothing in it can.**

**Stage 1 — on-page, no email required.** Single-page scorecard, one question per screen, progress bar. Number renders instantly with the four-leak breakdown. **No email gate before the number** — a defensive buyer won't trade their address for a number they haven't seen, and an ungated number is shareable into group chats, which is free distribution you cannot buy.

**Stage 2 — email gate, after the number.** One field: "Send me my Gate One Pack." Contents: (1) the **G1 Intake Form**, the literal on-screen artifact, ready to duplicate; (2) their **Leak Report PDF** — number, four-leak split, their answers, dated, the object they re-read at 11pm; (3) the **Data Boundary Map** — the anti-theft document, possibly the most valuable page in the pack for this buyer; (4) the **Negative Proof clip**, the 3:00 segment cut standalone.

**Deliberately NOT included:** G2 Quote logic, G3 Deposit gate, G4 Delivery. The audit tells them which gates are leaking and hands them exactly one. **That gap is the sale.**

### The 12 questions

Ranges or sliders, never blank boxes. Every question carries a "best guess is fine" microcopy line. *Blank boxes are where audits die.*

**Gate 1 — Intake**
1. How many commission or project inquiries do you get in a typical month? `0–1 / 2–3 / 4–6 / 7–12 / 13–25 / 25+`
2. What does a typical job invoice for? `$ slider, $100–$10,000`
3. Out of 10 serious inquiries, how many become paid jobs? `0–10`
4. From inquiry to your first reply — how long, honestly? `Under 4 hrs / 4–24 hrs / 1–3 days / 4–7 days / When I remember`

**Gate 2 — Quote**
5. From first reply to a firm price in their hands? `Same day / 1–2 days / 3–5 days / 6+ days / I avoid quoting until they push`
6. Last quarter, how many inquiries went silent after you replied? `0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6–10 / 10+`

**Gate 3 — Deposit**
7. Last quarter, how many jobs did you start before money was collected? `0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6+`
8. Of those, how many paid late, paid short, or never paid? `0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4+`

**Gate 4 — Delivery**
9. Last quarter, how many jobs ran past the agreed number of revisions? `0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6+`
10. On a job that overruns, how many unpaid extra hours does it eat? `1–2 / 3–5 / 6–10 / 10+`
11. What is your hourly floor? `$ slider, $15–$250` *("Don't know? Take question 2 and divide by your typical hours. That is your real rate, not your target one.")*
12. Last quarter, how many times did a client use your work in a way you hadn't priced — a print run, merch, ads, a brand's feed? `0 / 1 / 2 / 3+`

### The scoring math

Midpoints used. `Q` = quarterly inquiries (Q1 × 3) · `V` = Q2 · `c` = close rate (Q3 ÷ 10) · `h` = Q11.

**Leak 1 — Response lag.** `f₁` from Q4: Under 4hrs **0.00** · 4–24hrs **0.05** · 1–3 days **0.12** · 4–7 days **0.20** · When I remember **0.30**
`L1 = Q × c × V × f₁`

**Leak 2 — Quote lag (the ghost bucket).** `f₂` from Q5: Same day **0.00** · 1–2 days **0.10** · 3–5 days **0.25** · 6+ days **0.40** · I avoid it **0.50**
`L2 = Q6 × c × V × f₂`

**Leak 3 — No deposit gate.** `L3 = Q8 × V × 0.5`

**Leak 4 — Delivery.** `L4a = Q9 × Q10 × h` · `L4b = Q12 × V × 0.25` · `L4 = L4a + L4b`

**`TOTAL = L1 + L2 + L3 + L4`, rounded DOWN to the nearest $10.** Always understate — you never want a screenshot of this number to be arguable.

**Display:**
> ## Your desk lost you $3,180 last quarter.
> Gate 1 — Intake: **$640** · Gate 2 — Quote: **$1,290** · Gate 3 — Deposit: **$900** · Gate 4 — Delivery: **$350**
> *These are estimates built from your inputs. Not a promise, not a projection. The assumptions behind each line are shown below — check the math yourself.*

Every multiplier printed under a **"Show the math"** toggle. For a buyer whose dominant emotion is *you are trying to take something from me*, transparent arithmetic is the trust unlock.

**Bands:** `$0–499` HAIRLINE (your desk is tight, save your money) · `$500–1,499` LEAKING (one gate open, probably Gate 1) · `$1,500–3,999` DRAINING (multiple gates, compounds with volume) · `$4,000+` OPEN DRAIN (you are running a business on memory).

×  2.0%  →    200 CTA clicks

**Say that out loud, because it is the most important sentence in this document: one video does not fill this room.** A 14:55 niche video on a channel without an existing commission-artist audience does not do 83,000 views on release. Build the launch on that assumption and you will run a room with nine people in it and conclude the offer failed — when what failed was the arithmetic.

## COORDINATOR NOTES

**Runbook compliance:** agent jobs kept separate; each output connects to a monetization path; evidence status stated before finalizing.
